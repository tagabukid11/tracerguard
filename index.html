    Why Tracer exists

    Thousands of tokens launch on pump.fun every day. On the chart, many look organic. The first block tells a different story: a few wallets, often funded from the same source, buy half the supply before anyone else can click. They wait for real buyers, then dump. When people start checking, they move tokens to fresh wallets so the holder list looks clean.

    We built Tracer because we were tired of finding out too late. Most scanners hand you raw numbers. Tracer reads the launch block, follows the dev, follows bundle wallets even when they hide their tokens, and tells you what it means in plain words. It stays free, because everyone deserves to see the trap before they step in.

    What Tracer checks

    Every scan follows the token from its first block to today.

    Bundled launch buys
    Wallets that bought in the exact launch block, how much they took and how much they still hold.
    Snipers
    Fast buyers in the first few blocks after launch, often bots or insiders.
    Dev activity
    What the dev bought, sold or sent away, how fast they sold and how many other tokens they launched.
    Hidden transfers
    When bundle wallets move tokens to fresh wallets instead of selling, Tracer follows them and still counts that supply as held.
    Funding links
    Wallets that got their first SOL from the same source, a common sign of one person running many wallets.
    Liquidity and price
    What is left in the pool and how far the price is from its all-time high.
    Verdict
    "Major sell-off" means wallets that bought in the launch block sold 20% or more of the supply and the price fell 70% or more, or the pool was emptied. A dev wallet selling is shown on its own as "Large dev sale". Verdicts describe on-chain activity, not anyone's intent.
